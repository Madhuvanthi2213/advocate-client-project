General information to help you prepare. It is not legal advice for your specific situation.

Booking a consultation

How do I book a consultation?

Use the consultation request form on this page: choose your practice area, a preferred date and time, and add a short, general note. Your request will be confirmed after availability is checked. You can also reach the office using the contact details in the Contact section.

Does booking a request make me a client?

No. Submitting a request does not create an advocate–client relationship. Please avoid sharing urgent or highly confidential details in the form.

What to bring

What documents should I bring?

Bring a photo ID and any papers connected to your matter, such as notices, summons, court orders, agreements, and correspondence. Depending on the matter, this may also include the dishonoured cheque and bank return memo (cheque bounce), marriage and family records (family law), property and title documents (civil and property), the FIR or accident records (criminal and motor accident), or bills and warranty details (consumer). A short written timeline of events also helps.

Can I bring copies instead of originals?

Copies are usually enough for the first discussion. Keep the originals safe, as you may be asked to produce them later.

Online consultation

Is an online consultation available?

Consultations are arranged by appointment, and the advocate can advise on phone or email options. Mention in your request if you prefer a remote consultation, and the office will confirm what is possible for your matter and date.

In which languages can I consult?

Consultations are available in English and Tamil.

The legal process

Can you represent me in court?

Yes. The advocate practises at the District Court, Coimbatore, and represents clients in matters such as family, civil, consumer, cheque bounce, criminal and motor accident cases.

How are fees decided?

Fees depend on the nature and complexity of the matter and the work involved. Please enquire directly when you book a consultation.

I received a legal notice or court summons. What should I do?

Do not ignore it. Keep the original, note any dates mentioned, and bring it to your consultation as early as possible so your options and any deadlines can be reviewed.

How can I check the status of a court case?

Court case details can generally be searched on the e-Courts Services portal, or through the concerned court. A case number, or the party names, district and approximate year, help narrow the search.

How long will my case take?

Timelines vary widely with the type of case, the court, and the other side’s response. After reviewing your documents, the advocate can give you a realistic outline of the likely stages.
